## Problem

A B2B technology company needed a customer-facing chatbot that could answer
questions accurately from its own internal documentation, without hallucinating
answers or surfacing irrelevant context. Generic LLM responses weren't
acceptable — every answer had to be traceable back to a real source document.

## Approach

I treated retrieval quality as the actual product, not the language model. That
meant spending most of the effort on chunking strategy and retrieval tuning
rather than prompt tricks: how documents were split, how much overlap chunks
carried, and what similarity threshold counted as "relevant enough" to include
in context.

I optimized deliberately for precision over recall — a chatbot that says "I
don't know" is more trustworthy than one that answers confidently from a weak
match.

## Architecture

- Document ingestion and chunking pipeline feeding a vector store on Supabase
- Embedding and similarity search tuned against a relevance threshold, not a
  fixed top-k
- A prompt layer that injects only retrieved chunks above that threshold, with
  the model instructed to defer rather than guess when nothing qualifies
- A customer-facing chat interface wired to the retrieval and generation layer

## Result

Shipped as a working, customer-facing chat interface backed entirely by the
company's own documents, with retrieval tuned to prioritize correct answers
over broad coverage.

## What I'd do differently

I'd invest earlier in a retrieval evaluation harness — a small labeled set of
question/expected-source pairs — instead of tuning thresholds by feel. It would
have caught chunking regressions faster and made the accuracy-over-recall
tradeoff a measured decision instead of a judgment call.

## Problem

Most small businesses in Davao — carinderias, sari-sari stores, home bakers —
sell through Facebook posts and chat. They need a simple page with their
menu, prices, hours and a way to pay, that they can update themselves from a
phone on mobile data. Existing website builders are too heavy, too
expensive, or need a payment-gateway account a small business can't get.

## Approach

I designed for the slowest phone on the worst connection:

- The public page is server-rendered HTML with inline CSS and **zero
  JavaScript**, about 3 KB gzipped, with WebP images that lazy-load
- Owners pay nothing extra: they upload their **own** GCash, Maya or QR Ph
  code and customers pay them directly
- The owner admin works without JavaScript, and a small script shrinks phone
  photos *before* upload so they go through on slow data
- Deliberately not built yet: online ordering. Customers already message the
  owner to order, so I'm waiting for pilots to ask for it

## Architecture

- Node.js + Express, server-rendered pages
- PostgreSQL: Supabase in production, PGlite (real Postgres, in-process)
  locally, so there's nothing to install
- Images processed with Sharp; the payment QR is kept lossless so it stays
  scannable
- Open Graph tags for Facebook link previews and schema.org LocalBusiness data
- Security: scrypt passwords, hashed session tokens in HttpOnly cookies,
  login throttling, cross-origin POST rejection, a strict Content Security
  Policy
- CLI scripts to onboard a business, which starts hidden until the owner
  previews and publishes it

## Result

A working platform with a demo storefront, ready for pilot businesses, that
runs on free hosting tiers until there's a paying client.

## What I'd do differently

I'd budget for paid hosting from the first pilot. The free tiers work, but the
web service sleeps after 15 minutes without traffic and the first visit then
takes about a minute — a bad first impression for a customer standing at the
counter. An uptime ping keeps it awake, but that's a workaround, not a plan.

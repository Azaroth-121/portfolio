## Problem

The existing marketing site was slow, its information architecture didn't
match how prospects actually searched, and leads captured through its forms
weren't reliably reaching the CRM.

## Approach

I started from search intent and page speed as the two non-negotiables, then
worked backward into information architecture: what pages should exist, how
they should link to each other, and what structured data would let search
engines and AI answer engines understand the page without guessing.

<MediaPlaceholder label="Site architecture / sitemap diagram" />

## Architecture

- WordPress build with an information architecture organized around real
  search intent rather than internal org structure
- Structured data (schema.org markup) across key page types
- Page-speed work: image optimization, render-blocking cleanup, caching
- Lead capture forms wired directly into the CRM so no submission gets lost

<MediaPlaceholder label="Page speed before/after" />

## Result

A rebuilt marketing site with faster page loads, an information architecture
that matches how prospects search, and lead capture that reliably reaches the
CRM.

## What I'd do differently

I'd set up page-speed monitoring as part of the build itself, not as a
post-launch check — content teams add images and embeds over time, and without
a budget enforced at publish time, speed erodes quietly.
